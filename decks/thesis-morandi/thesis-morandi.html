AI Disclosure

and Consumer Purchase Intention in Fashion Advertising

Part 01

Introduction

Background

Generative AI now makes the ad

Midjourney, DALL·E and Stable Diffusion produce photoreal fashion campaigns at a fraction of the cost.

Regulators are moving to mandatory labels

The EU, UK and US are heading toward compulsory disclosure of AI-generated content.

But consumer response is barely understood

Does revealing an ad is AI-made change what people buy? Evidence is thin — and Western-centric.

Objective & Research Question

The gap

Consumer reactions to AI disclosure are under-studied outside Western markets.

The objective

Test whether disclosure lowers purchase intention — and uncover the mechanism and boundaries.

Research question

Does disclosing that a fashion ad is AI-generated reduce purchase intention — and if so, why and for whom?

Two Theoretical Lenses

Persuasion Knowledge Model

Friestad & Wright, 1994

Consumers draw on persuasion knowledge to cope with marketer tactics. A disclosure label can act as a cue that activates this coping — inviting scepticism.

Stimulus – Organism – Response

Mehrabian & Russell, 1974

A stimulus (the disclosure) shifts internal states — perceived authenticity, persuasion knowledge — which drive the response: purchase intention.

Five Hypotheses

H1 · Disclosure lowers purchase intention

H2 · …mediated by perceived authenticity

H3 · …mediated by persuasion knowledge

H4 · Effect is stronger for hedonic products

H5 · AI literacy weakens the effect

Part 04

Discussion

Why No Effect?

1 · Chinese sample — higher baseline acceptance of AI

2 · Weak contrast — half of controls already assumed AI

3 · Mid-market sneaker — no luxury heritage to threaten

4 · Consistent with recent nulls (Koning & Voorveld, 2025)

Powered only for medium-to-large effects — the null bounds an effect rather than excluding it.

What It Means

Limits: convenience sample · single product · cross-sectional → next: EU & luxury samples, longitudinal

Practical

In high-AI-literacy markets, disclosure may carry little sales risk. Invest in authenticity, not in hiding the label.

Theoretical

AI-disclosure effects are context-dependent. Western-sample findings should not be over-generalised.

How, not whether

The question is not whether to disclose AI — but how to preserve authenticity.
